# Miami Paradise Studio

register: brand

## Product purpose

A three-page site for a pre-launch game studio. It has nothing to sell yet: no Steam page, no Discord, no build. Its entire job is to make a small studio read as real and competent to two audiences who will judge it in about four seconds.

## Users

**The scout.** A player or press contact who followed a link, knows nothing about the studio, and will decide from the first fold whether SHARD Protocol sounds like a game or like vapour. Wants to know what the game is, in one sentence, before scrolling.

**The evaluator.** An investor, publisher or potential collaborator checking whether the team has a method. Skims for evidence of process and honest status, and is repelled by unbacked claims.

Both arrive skeptical. Both have seen a hundred pre-launch studio pages that promised a revolution and shipped nothing.

## Tone

Blunt and instrumented. The studio talks like an operator, not a marketer: it names its own gates, publishes its own kill criteria, and marks unfinished things as unfinished. Confidence comes from specificity, never from adjectives.

Every "coming soon" on this site is a promise not yet kept. The design should treat those as honest status, not as decoration to hide.

## Brand voice, as physical objects

Hard-edged. Humid. Instrumented.

The reference objects are a Miami arcade marquee at 3am and a military field manual printed on cheap paper. Heat and equipment. Not chrome, not clean sci-fi.

## Anti-references

- Generic cyberpunk: cyan-on-black, Orbitron, circuit-board textures, "hacker" green terminals. The first thing anyone reaches for in this category.
- SaaS landing page grammar: three identical icon cards, a big-number stat row, a gradient headline, a soft purple mesh background.
- Esports team-page maximalism: angular shards over every element, italic all-caps everything, seven gradients.
- Anything that reads as certain about a product that does not exist yet.

## Strategic principles

1. **Status is the content.** A studio in vertical slice that says so beats one pretending to be in beta. Design the unfinished states properly instead of hiding them.
2. **The game must be legible in one fold.** 4v4, capture flags, breach the base, voice commands. If a visitor leaves knowing only "cyberpunk shooter", the page failed.
3. **Evidence over adjectives.** Phases have durations. Metrics have names. Nothing claims a number the studio cannot show.
4. **Heat, not chrome.** The world is humid and sodium-lit, not blue and sterile. That is the single decision that separates this from every other neon page.
